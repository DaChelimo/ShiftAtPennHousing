Floating is the system's answer to a desk with nobody on it. Rather than paying a
contracted guard, it looks for a worker who is already on shift somewhere with staff to
spare, sends them over for the uncovered stretch, and sends them back.

This page is the whole idea, without the algorithm. If you want to know **why the system
picked you**, that is the [deep dive](/system/floating/deep-dive).

## The three facts

Almost every question about floating is answered by one of these.

1. **Floating never adds hours.** It relocates hours you were already scheduled to work. A
   Quad worker on 19:00 to 24:00 who floats to Lauder from 21:00 to 22:00 still works five
   hours: two at Quad, one at Lauder, two at Quad.
2. **Your own desk is never left empty to cover someone else's.** A worker is only
   floatable if their home desk still has at least one person on it after they leave. This
   is a hard guard, not a preference.
3. **Only Quad and Harnwell workers can be floated out, and nobody can be floated in to
   Harnwell.** Quad and Harnwell are the multi-staffed desks, so they are the only ones
   with anyone to spare. Harnwell can send but never receive, because working the Harnwell
   desk requires training that only Harnwell workers have.

The lookup only runs if the desk would be **completely empty**. A desk that still has one
worker on it is covered, even if its schedule calls for two or three. See

## What you have to do

**Acknowledge it.** The deadline is 10 minutes before the float starts, and reminders
escalate as it approaches: 6 hours, 2 hours, 1 hour, 30 minutes, and 5 minutes before the
deadline. A float assigned at the usual two-hours-out lookup gets the last three of those.

You can also **decline**, and declining is a real, distinct action rather than a polite way
of ignoring it. Declining voids the float immediately, puts the seat back in the open feed,
and takes you out of consideration for that particular gap. It is better than silence for
everyone, because it lets the system start looking again straight away.

<Callout label="Ignoring it is not the same as declining">
  A float you never answer stays assigned to you until the deadline, and the desk is
  counting on it the whole time. If you cannot work it, decline it.
</Callout>

## Rules that constrain the outcome

- **No takeback.** Once a float is assigned, automation will not recall you, even if your
  home house later runs short. The destination is owed that coverage. Only a manager can
  undo a float by hand.
- **Hours caps are not consulted.** Floats are hours-neutral, so a worker already at 19
  hours is still floatable. Claims, swaps, and pickups do add hours and are capped. See
  [Hours, caps, and attribution](/system/hours-rules).
- **A manager can force one early.** If a house's manager already knows nobody local will
  claim a shift, they can run the lookup ahead of time rather than waiting for the two-hour
  step.
- **A float can be shared.** One gap covered by two floaters is a normal outcome, not a
  failure. Each floater gets their own assignment for their own stretch.
