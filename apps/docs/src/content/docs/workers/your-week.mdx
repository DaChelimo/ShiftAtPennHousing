My Shifts opens first because it is the answer to the only question most people open the
app for. It is your week as a running list: one day after another, with what you are
working on each one.

## Do it

<Steps>
  <li>
    Open the app. My Shifts is the first tab.
    <span class="step-note">On the web portal it is the home page after you sign in.</span>
  </li>
  <li>
    Check the week navigator, above the tab bar, to confirm which week you are looking at.
  </li>
  <li>
    Move between weeks with its arrows, or tap it to jump to a specific week.
    <span class="step-note">
      A shift you pick up for a future week appears under that week, not this one.
    </span>
  </li>
  <li>
    Tap a shift to manage it.
    <span class="step-note">
      One sheet handles both giving it up and trading it. See
      <a href="/workers/dropping">Dropping a shift</a> and
      <a href="/workers/swapping">Swapping and handing off</a>.
    </span>
  </li>
</Steps>

## What you'll see

Each day is its own heading, with the number of shifts and the hours on it. A day you are
not working says so rather than being left out, so the week reads as a week and not as a
list of exceptions.

Once the week is underway, the days that have already passed fold into a single collapsed
**Earlier this week** card. Today sits near the top of the screen instead of below
yesterday. Tap it to open the days back up.

The screen also shows the **hours you hold in the week you are looking at**, against your
cap. That total follows the week you navigate to, so it is not always this week's.

### The badges on a card

<Table>

| Badge | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Picked up | You claimed this yourself rather than being scheduled for it. |
| Swap request | Somebody has asked to trade with you and is waiting on your answer. |
| Swap pending | You asked to trade and are waiting on theirs. |
| A house name | The shift is at a house that is not your own, either a pickup or a float. |

</Table>

A shift card is tinted with the colour of whoever holds it, so a week grid reads as people
rather than as blocks. The states below override that, because a state means something the
colour has to carry.

<StateList>
  <StateCard state="float-out" label="Floating out">
    You are scheduled here, but part of this shift is being worked at another desk.
  </StateCard>
  <StateCard state="float-in" label="Floating in">
    You are covering another house for this stretch. It is not extra hours.
  </StateCard>
  <StateCard state="pending" label="Awaiting your acknowledgment">
    A float has been assigned to you and the app is waiting on your answer.
  </StateCard>
</StateList>

## Rules that apply

- **The week navigator scopes the whole screen**, including the hours total.
- **A shift you drop leaves this screen.** It becomes an opening in the Open Shifts tab,
  where anyone at the house can claim it, including you. There is no separate reclaim: you
  take it back the same way anybody else would. See [Dropping a shift](/workers/dropping).
- **The screen updates itself.** A swap your partner just accepted, or a float just
  assigned to you, appears without a pull to refresh.
- **Hours worked at another house count toward the same weekly total.** See
  [Your hours](/workers/hours).

## If it goes wrong

<Callout label="A shift is missing">
  Check the week navigator first. A shift picked up for next week is filed under next week,
  which is the single most common reason a shift seems to disappear. If it was earlier
  today, open the **Earlier this week** card.
</Callout>

**A shift you dropped is gone from your week entirely.** That is expected. It is no longer
yours. Look for it in Open Shifts, where it now sits as an opening.

**A card shows a house that is not yours.** You either picked that shift up at that house
or you were floated there. A float carries its own label; a pickup just names the house.
