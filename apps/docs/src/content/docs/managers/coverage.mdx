This page is a runbook. Read the boxed rule, then the three rungs, then the close-out
outcomes. Everything else is detail you can come back for.

<Callout label="The one rule" critical>
  **An open coverage request never clears itself.** When its window passes without a
  close-out, it goes overdue and stays visible until a human records what happened. It does
  not archive, expire, or quietly resolve. If you acknowledge it and walk away, it is still
  yours.
</Callout>

## What the alert means

A coverage request is opened at the moment the automated chain runs out of internal
options: the block was broadcast, nobody claimed it, and the float lookup found no eligible
worker. Unless somebody procures Allied, that desk will be empty.

One request covers one contiguous uncovered stretch at one house. The chain fires once
every 30 minutes, so adjacent blocks are merged into a single request rather than paging
you eight times for one empty evening. Merging never re-pages.

## The three rungs

The request escalates on its own until somebody acknowledges it.

    {

      who: 'Housing Manager on duty',
      what: 'Terminal. Nobody is above this rung, so it stays here and keeps reminding.',
      state: 'urgent',

/>

A rung with nobody reachable is **skipped immediately** rather than holding the request for
its full hour. If all three are unreachable the request falls to the project administrator.
It is never fanned out to any other manager, and never to another house's RSM.

The hold and reminder intervals are configurable, and default to 60 and 15 minutes.

## Acknowledge and close out are different

They are separate on purpose, and the difference is the whole point of the surface.

<Table>

| Action | What it means | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Acknowledge | I have seen this and I am handling it. | Stops the ladder and the reminders. The request stays open. |
| Close out | Here is what actually happened. | Removes the request from the active view. Requires an outcome. |

</Table>

**Any manager who can build for that house may acknowledge**, not only the rung currently
holding it. An RSM picking up a request that has already passed to their HM is a good
outcome, not an error.

### The four close-out outcomes

<Steps>
  <li>
    <strong>Allied secured.</strong> You called Allied and the window is covered.
  </li>
  <li>
    <strong>Covered internally.</strong> A worker picked it up, or you assigned somebody by
    hand.
  </li>
  <li>
    <strong>Desk went unstaffed.</strong> Nobody covered it.
    <span class="step-note">
      Requires a written note, and is recorded as a missed-coverage incident.
    </span>
  </li>
  <li>
    <strong>No longer needed.</strong> The window stopped existing, or the desk regained a
    worker.
  </li>
</Steps>

The system records one of these on its own in exactly one case: the block was voided by a
configuration change, or the desk got a worker back. Everything else is your call.

## Do it

<Steps>
  <li>Open the alert from the banner. It is on every page of the console while anything is open.</li>
  <li>
    Acknowledge it, so the ladder stops paging and your colleagues can see it is being
    handled.
  </li>
  <li>Call Allied, or find internal coverage.</li>
  <li>
    Come back and close it out with what actually happened.
    <span class="step-note">This is the step that gets skipped. It is the one that matters.</span>
  </li>
</Steps>

## Rules that apply

- **Coverage is secured at most four hours at a time.** A longer empty window is never sent
  to Allied all at once. The later hours stay claimable and re-escalate on their own, so
  students are not locked out of hours that were still fillable.
- **Securing Allied does not re-open the seats.** Once an empty desk reaches the two-hour
  step, its vacant seats stay locked, whether a floater or Allied fills it. Nobody piles
  onto a window that has already been paid for.
- **A desk with one worker on it is not a coverage failure.** The chain only fires for a
  block that would have nobody present. Empty seats on a still-staffed desk stay quietly
  claimable. See [How a shift gets covered](/system/coverage-ladder).
- **Every unstaffed close-out and every overdue request** appears in the coverage report
  for review.

## If it goes wrong

<Callout label="The alert will not load">
  You are told that explicitly, at the same prominence as a real alert. It never resolves
  to an all-clear. Silence on this surface means every desk is accounted for, so a failed
  read is never allowed to look like quiet. Treat it as unknown coverage and check the
  house schedule directly.
</Callout>

**You acknowledged something that was not yours.** That is fine and it is by design.
Acknowledge stops the ladder; it does not transfer ownership away from anyone who can act.

**A request you closed came back.** It did not. A new stretch at the same house opens its
own request. Check the coverage window on the card.
